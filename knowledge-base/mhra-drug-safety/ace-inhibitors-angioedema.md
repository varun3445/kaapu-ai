# ACE-Inhibitors: Bradykinin- vs Histamine-Mediated Angioedema

Angioedema is a known uncommon or rare side-effect of ACE inhibitor treatment that may be either allergic (histamine-mediated) or non-allergic (bradykinin-mediated).

## Key Clinical Points

- Angioedema can develop at any time, including after weeks to years of use
- Bradykinin-mediated cases do not reliably respond to standard anaphylaxis treatment
- When standard treatments fail, consider bradykinin-mediated mechanisms as a cause
- If angioedema is suspected, discontinue the ACE inhibitor immediately and do not restart

## Patient Guidance

- Swelling of face, lips, tongue, or throat may occur despite prolonged use without previous issues
- Seek urgent medical attention if you develop swelling, or breathing/swallowing difficulties
- Stop taking the medication if angioedema is suspected
- Inform healthcare providers of any prior angioedema history

## Data Context

Roughly half of angioedema cases occurred 30+ days after treatment initiation, with fatal airway compromise cases documented after prolonged use.
