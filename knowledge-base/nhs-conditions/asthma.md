# Asthma

## Symptoms
Wheezing, coughing, shortness of breath, chest tightness. Fluctuates, often worse at night/early morning. Triggers: exercise, air pollution, cold temperatures, allergens (pollen, dust, mould, pet dander).

## Causes
Airways become swollen and narrower, restricting airflow. Risk factors: family history of asthma/allergies, premature birth, low birth weight, childhood pollution/smoke exposure, previous respiratory infections (bronchiolitis, croup), being overweight, workplace chemical exposure.

## When to See a GP
Symptoms occurring, current treatment not working, using inhalers more frequently than usual, symptoms preventing normal activities or disrupting sleep.

## Emergency (call 999)
During an asthma attack: worsening symptoms, no improvement after maximum reliever doses, no inhaler available. If unimproved after 10 minutes, repeat inhaler dose. After recovery, get an urgent GP appointment within 2 days.

## Treatment
Inhalers are primary treatment: anti-inflammatory reliever (AIR), maintenance and reliever therapy (MART), or separate preventer/blue reliever inhalers. For inadequate control: stronger inhalers, tablets (montelukast), or biologic injectable therapies.

## Self-Care
Always carry inhaler, use medications as prescribed, identify and avoid personal triggers, exercise regularly with medical guidance, maintain healthy weight, get vaccinated (especially flu), avoid smoking, check with healthcare provider before using other medications.
