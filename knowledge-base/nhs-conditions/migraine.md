# Migraine

## Symptoms
Throbbing headache, often on one side, with nausea and light sensitivity. Early warning signs: fatigue, food cravings, mood changes, neck stiffness. Some experience aura (visual disturbances, tingling) before the headache. Attacks typically last 4 hours to 3 days.

## Causes
Exact cause unknown — involves changes to nerves and blood vessels in the brain. Genetic factor; more common in women. Triggers: menstrual cycles, stress, skipped meals, excessive caffeine, anxiety, insufficient exercise.

## When to See a GP
Attacks that are severe, worsening, lasting longer than usual, occurring more than weekly, or difficult to control. Also migraine before/during period.

## Urgent (call 111)
Migraine lasting over 72 hours, aura symptoms exceeding 1 hour, attacks during pregnancy/postpartum.

## Emergency (call 999)
Sudden severe headache, speech problems, vision loss, confusion, seizures, high fever with meningitis symptoms, one-sided weakness.

## Treatment
Cannot be cured, but managed with painkillers, triptans, anti-nausea medication, beta-blockers, antidepressants, acupuncture, CBT. Avoid overusing painkillers — limit to 2 days/week.

## Self-Care
Rest in darkened room, avoid known triggers, stay hydrated, limit caffeine/alcohol, regular meal times, regular exercise, prioritize sleep, manage stress.
